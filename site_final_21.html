Reading these: features are ranked by how often the real trees split on them — a direct measure of what the model actually leans on, computed from the exported model itself. The top slot in three of four models is revenue_intensity, the feature flagged separately as target leakage (it's built from the proposed quote, which doesn't exist before an RFP is priced) — its distribution below shows why: it separates Booked from Lost almost too well to be an honest predictor.
Kim-specified vs. engineered: every card now shows whether the feature traces back to a parameter in Kim's "APIT Parameters by Data Source" doc (Cvent, the CRM, PMS, the RMS/forecast tool, and her own flags-surfaced-to-user list) — hover the ⓘ for the exact bullet she wrote. That document names business-level data-source parameters, not ML column names, so this is a semantic match I made by hand, not a literal tag anywhere in the codebase — a handful of matches (like which field means "group type") are judgment calls, noted case by case. 40 of the 69 features trace to something she explicitly named; the other 29 are ratios, rolling-window stats, EMAs, velocities, cyclical encodings, or composite scores the pipeline engineers on top of (or beyond) her named inputs.
