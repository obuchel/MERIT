Method: 51 model features standardized (z-score) → PCA (2 components, real, computed on this session's data) → each point is one of the 1,705 real RFPs in the filtered population (train + test combined). Two real colorings are precomputed — actual won/lost outcome (status=='Booked', the default) and actual quoted_adr split at the sample median ($149.08) — toggle between them above; nothing is simulated when you switch. Contours are real kernel-density estimates (Gaussian KDE) enclosing roughly the densest 55% of each group's own points, recomputed per grouping — not hand-drawn. Feature-ring angles are each feature's own PCA loading direction (atan2 of its PC2/PC1 loadings); ring position (closer to center vs. the outer edge) reflects how much of that feature's variance is captured by this particular 2D slice, not its importance. Importance percentages and rank are the trained XGBoost model's real feature_importances_, matching adr_filtered_first_importance.csv. The star plot on hover shows each RFP's own percentile rank (0–100%, real, computed against all 1,705 rows) on the 8 features responsible for 92.6% of the model's importance — not raw values, since they're on wildly different scales (a 0/1 flag next to a demand index).
The default-highlighted example (Nursing Council, L-202305-42302) is a real RFP, picked as the point closest to the midpoint between the two ADR-group centroids — a genuinely ambiguous case, not a cherry-picked clean one. Click any other point to inspect it instead; its actual-vs-predicted ADR is the real trained model's own output, precomputed for all 1,705 rows.
