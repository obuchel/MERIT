Cook's-distance over-flagging in the ADR funnel page

Every one of the 298 candidate-pool features gets its own simple linear-regression scatter against quoted_adr, with points flagged as outliers when Cook's distance exceeds the standard 4/n threshold. Recomputed here directly from the funnel page's own embedded JSON, across all 298 features and all 1705 plotted rows (1702 scored + 3 excluded-from-training rows shown out-of-sample) — same formula the page runs client-side, just applied to every feature at once instead of one at a time as a person clicks through.

Flag count vs. actual price

Each dot is one RFP: its real quoted_adr on the x-axis, and how many of the 298 feature-scatters flag it as an outlier on the y-axis. If Cook's distance were finding genuine anomalies, this would look like scattered noise. Instead it's close to deterministic: rows near the typical price band get flagged by almost nothing, and flag count rises sharply the further a row's price sits from the middle — because an extreme y produces a large residual against nearly any x, correlated or not.

Extreme-priced rows vs. typical rows

Same data, summarized: rows in the top/bottom 5% of price get flagged roughly 20× as often as everyone else.

Which features do most of the flagging

The worst offenders aren't the features most related to price — they're weak or near-categorical ones, where almost any nonzero residual clears the bar. Four separate calendar encodings of the same date (month, day-of-year, quarter, week-of-year) each independently flag ~9% of all rows.
