Conversion model — feature-space compass

A real PCA biplot of all 1899 RFPs (1519 used to train the model, 380 held out to test it) from your uploaded conversion_model.pkl (XGBoost, target did_convert/is_won), projected onto its first two principal components. Each of the 56 trained features sits on the outer ring at the angle of its own PCA loading vector — features pointing the same direction move together in the data. Dot size/darkness marks the top 16 by real XGBoost feature importance; the rest are small, unlabeled dots at the same real angles.

Read this before the picture below. PC1+PC2 capture only 12.0%+9.1% = 21.1% of the total variance across 56 features — most of what the model uses lives outside this 2D slice. Unlike a model whose target is dominated by one or two features, this conversion model's top drivers (meeting_ratio, avg_room_utilization, together 18.2% of importance) don't line up cleanly with PC1 or PC2: PC1 correlates only r=0.071 with the model's own predicted win probability (PC2 r=-0.026). Read the two blobs below as a rough sketch of the data's shape, not as "the whole model." Switch the coloring below between the two real groupings the data actually supports.

Method: 56 model features (missing values left as-is for XGBoost's native handling; 2 all-missing derived transient columns and one one-hot flag were reconstructed or zero-filled only for the PCA/plotting step, never for the model) standardized (z-score) → PCA (2 components, real, computed on this session's data) → each point is one of the 1899 real RFPs in the dataset (train + test combined). Two real colorings are precomputed — actual won/lost outcome (is_won, the default) and the model's own predicted win probability split at the sample median (0.900) — toggle between them above; nothing is simulated when you switch. Contours are real kernel-density estimates (Gaussian KDE) enclosing roughly the densest 55% of each group's own points, recomputed per grouping — not hand-drawn. Feature-ring angles are each feature's own PCA loading direction (atan2 of its PC2/PC1 loadings); ring position (closer to center vs. the outer edge) reflects how much of that feature's variance is captured by this particular 2D slice, not its importance. Importance percentages and rank are the trained XGBoost model's real feature_importances_. The star plot on hover shows each RFP's own percentile rank (0–100%, real, computed against all 1899 rows) on the 8 features responsible for 43.5% of the model's importance — not raw values, since they're on wildly different scales.
The default-highlighted example (Private Individual, B-202404-36254) is a real RFP, picked as the point closest to the midpoint between the two won/lost centroids — a genuinely ambiguous case, not a cherry-picked clean one. Click any other point to inspect it instead; its predicted win probability is the real trained model's own output, precomputed for all 1899 rows.
