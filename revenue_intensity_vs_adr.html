The raw correlation is misleading. Computed over all 1,899 RFPs, Pearson r = −0.64 — it looks like higher revenue intensity predicts a lower rate. But Spearman ρ (rank-based, less sensitive to outliers) is only +0.03, nearly zero — a big disagreement between the two that's itself a signal something's off. The cause: 194 meeting-only RFPs (room_block = 0) have quoted_adr pinned at exactly $0 (correct — no rooms were booked), while their revenue_intensity is computed as all-F&B/meeting revenue divided by 1 room-night (the code's zero-guard), producing values up to $48,323. That combination — huge x, zero y — is what drags Pearson negative; it's a unit-mismatch artifact, not a real pricing relationship.
Filtered to room-based RFPs, the real relationship is positive. This matches room_block > 0, the exact filter rfp_xgboost_pipeline_v7.py already applies before training the ADR model (its own "FIX 1", documented in the script's header). On that 1,705-row subset: Pearson r = +0.42, Spearman ρ = +0.42 — the two agree now, which is itself evidence the relationship is genuinely monotonic once the artifact is removed. Groups that generate more total revenue per room-night do tend to get quoted a higher room rate, which is the direction you'd expect.
